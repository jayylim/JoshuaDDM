**Academic source:** Yeung & Summerfield (2012), *Metacognition in human decision-making: confidence and error monitoring*.

The paper provides the following **qualitative bases for DDM parameters**:

* **Drift rate ((d)) — evidence quality / strength**

  * Represents the **rate at which evidence accumulates** toward a decision. 
  * **Stronger/clearer evidence → higher absolute drift rate.**
  * **Greater perceptual ambiguity/difficulty → lower drift rate.**
  * The paper specifically characterizes errors arising from perceptual ambiguity as associated with **low (d)**. 
  * **Prior implication:** conditions with better evidence can be expected *a priori* to have higher drift rates than conditions with poorer evidence.

* **Decision boundary / threshold ((u)) — response caution**

  * Represents the **amount of accumulated evidence required before committing to a choice**. 
  * **Higher boundary → more cautious responding**, favouring accuracy over speed.
  * **Lower boundary → greater urgency / faster responding**, at the cost of more errors.
  * The paper explicitly associates errors under decision urgency with **low (u)**. 
  * **Prior implication:** accuracy-emphasis conditions should have higher boundaries than speed-emphasis conditions.

* **Post-error boundary adjustment — increased caution**

  * After detecting an error or response uncertainty, participants may **increase the decision boundary on subsequent trials**.
  * This produces slower, more cautious responding (“post-error slowing”). 
  * **Prior implication:** if modelling trial history, one could hypothesize (u_{\text{post-error}} > u_{\text{post-correct}}).

* **Starting point / origin of accumulation — response bias**

  * The paper notes that the **origin of accumulation can vary across trials** and that this contributes to explaining RT patterns. 
  * However, **it does not provide a substantive account of what experimental factors should determine starting-point bias**.
  * **Prior implication:** this paper provides little basis for specifying anything beyond allowing starting-point variation/bias where theoretically appropriate.

* **Diffusion/noise parameter ((c)) — stochastic variability**

  * Evidence accumulation contains **Gaussian noise**, (cW), with zero mean and variance determined by (c^2). 
  * However, the paper **does not give qualitative experimental predictions for how (c) should differ between conditions**.
  * **Prior implication:** it defines the role of noise but does not provide a useful empirical basis for condition-specific priors on it.

* **Non-decision time ((t_0))**

  * **Not substantively specified in this paper.**
  * Therefore, this paper provides **no qualitative basis for setting a non-decision-time prior**.

* **Confidence — joint relationship with drift and boundary**

  * The paper reviews theories proposing that confidence depends on an **interaction between evidence quality and evidence quantity**, including the proposal that confidence scales with (d\times u). 
  * However, the authors also discuss limitations of explaining confidence solely through standard DDM parameters.
  * **Prior implication:** useful for theorizing parameter–confidence relationships, but **not for assigning specific parameter values based on confidence**.

### Most defensible qualitative prior expectations from this paper


For **starting point, diffusion/noise, and non-decision time**, this particular paper gives **insufficient qualitative evidence for informative prior specification**.
